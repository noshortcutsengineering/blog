Steering with actual knowledge

Created 2026-08-06 · Updated 2026-08-06

I have been in software development since I finished my degree in systems engineering. Lately I have been thinking about switching toward machine learning. The idea is not new. It has been sitting there for years, fed by how much of the field now runs through models, data, and inference.

I already took a step: a master’s in big data, where I learned the basics and some of the harder AI material. That was useful. It was not a career switch. Knowing the map is different from walking the road every day.

Why now

It feels like the right time to make an actual move. Not a rebrand, not a weekend of tutorials, but sustained study. I am starting the Deep Learning Specialization from DeepLearning.AI and treating it as real work, not background noise.

It might lead nowhere career-wise. That is fine. Learning still holds. What changes is how you see problems afterward: what you can question, what you refuse to hand-wave, what you can rebuild from first principles when a tool fails.

Why deep study matters more with GenAI

Generative AI and agents make it easier than ever to skip the foundation. You can get an answer without knowing why it is right, or when it is wrong. That is useful for speed. It is dangerous if speed is all you have.

Someone still has to steer. Steering needs judgment, and judgment needs knowledge you earned the slow way: reading the material, doing the exercises, sitting with the math until it stops being theater. Shortcuts around that look efficient until you have to debug a system you never understood.

So I am going deep on purpose. Let’s see how it goes.
